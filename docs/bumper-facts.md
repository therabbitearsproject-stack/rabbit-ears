# Behind the bumpers

Each station has its own short story, with its source linked below. Copy is paraphrased for a short on-screen reading window.

## 2 · Ad Break

**Eighty-four miles of bunny thread.**

Energizer's giant Bunny balloon needed 84 miles of thread and size-98 feet. The battery-ad mascot became an airborne craft project on an absurd scale.

Source: [Energizer](https://energizer.com/energizer-bunny/bunny-timeline/)

## 3 · Rerun Alley

**Four Minutes, One Famous Fall**

Dick Van Dyke's famous ottoman opening was filmed in about four minutes. Carl Reiner requested a second version where he dodged it, keeping viewers guessing.

Source: [PBS American Masters / John Rich interview](https://video.wttw.com/video/what-made-the-opening-credits-of-the-dick-van-dyke-show-special-vvlegk/)

## 4 · Late Night

**The Pencil That Could Only Erase**

Johnny Carson had pencils specially made with erasers on both ends for The Tonight Show. Even his desk stationery had its own comedy routine.

Source: [Museum of Broadcast Communications](https://www.museum.tv/gift-shop/p/carson-pencil)

## 5 · Big Prize

**The Answers Came First**

On a plane, Julann Griffin proposed giving contestants the answers. Her husband Merv tested it with 5,280; the question was feet in a mile.

Source: [Television Academy / Tony Griffin interview](https://www.televisionacademy.com/features/news/hall-fame/merv-griffin-hall-fame-tribute)

## 6 · Brit TV

**Basil Was Someone’s Actual Boss**

John Cleese based Basil Fawlty on real hotelier Donald Sinclair, whose behavior included correcting guests' table manners. A disastrous hotel stay became a classic sitcom.

Source: [RTÉ / John Cleese](https://www.rte.ie/entertainment/2003/1008/400206-fawltytowers/)

## 7 · PBS

**His Mother Was the Wardrobe Department**

The Smithsonian's Mister Rogers cardigan still carries his mother's name inside: Nancy knitted it herself, just as she made sweaters he wore on television.

Source: [Smithsonian National Museum of American History](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_680637)

## 8 · Y2K WB

**All That Talking Filled Extra Pages**

Gilmore Girls burned through 77–78-page scripts; its showrunner said the crew shot nine or ten pages daily to keep up with all that conversation.

Source: [David S. Rosenthal interview / BuddyTV](https://www.buddytv.com/exclusive-interview-david-s-rosenthal-head-writer-and-executive-producer-on-gilmore-girls/)

## 9 · Porchlight

**The Moose Had a University Job**

The moose strolling through Northern Exposure's opening credits was Morty, a Washington State University research animal with a second career in television.

Source: [Washington State University](https://timeline.wsu.edu/timeline/marty-the-moose/)

## 10 · Prime Time Tapes

**A Fake Ending Guarded the Real One**

To protect Newhart's surprise finale, a fake ending sent his character to heaven. Suzanne Pleshette stayed hidden until the secret final scene was filmed.

Source: [Television Academy Interviews](https://interviews.televisionacademy.com/shows/newhart-the-last-newhart)

## 11 · Strange Tales

**The Theme Was a Splice**

The Twilight Zone's famous theme joined two separate pieces from CBS's music library. Marius Constant had written them as short fragments, never as that signature tune.

Source: [Rod Serling Memorial Foundation](https://rodserling.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Serling_Newsletter_Spring-2023-Final.pdf)

## 12 · Sitcom Gold

**Two Babies, One Broadcast Day**

Lucy Ricardo's baby arrived on television the same day Lucille Ball delivered Desi Jr. Her real birth was coordinated with the broadcast through a planned cesarean.

Source: [Television Academy / Desi Arnaz Jr. interview](https://www.televisionacademy.com/features/news/online-originals/heres-50-years-heres-lucy-part-1)

## 13 · Spooky '90s

**Scully Recruited Beyond the Screen**

In a 2018 survey, half the women familiar with Dana Scully said the X-Files character increased their interest in science, technology, engineering or mathematics.

Source: [Geena Davis Institute / 2018 Scully Effect study](https://geenadavisinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/x-files-scully-effect-report-geena-davis-institute.pdf)

## 14 · Order Now

**Thirty Thousand Pounds of Turkey**

Ron Popeil said developing his Olive Oil Fryer consumed 30,000 pounds of turkey; much of the testing food went to firefighters and homeless people.

Source: [Ron Popeil interview / University of Illinois Alumni Association](https://uiaa.org/2013/12/13/alumni-interview-ron-popeil/)

## 15 · Paint Along

**The Perm He Couldn’t Escape**

Bob Ross got a perm to save money on haircuts, then grew tired of it. By then, his curly-haired portrait had become the company logo.

Source: [NPR / Annette Kowalski interview](https://www.northcountrypublicradio.org/news/npr/490923502/the-real-bob-ross-meet-the-meticulous-artist-behind-those-happy-trees)

## 16 · Variety Hour

**Elvis Arrived, Ed Was Missing**

Elvis's first Ed Sullivan Show appearance happened without Ed Sullivan. Recovering from a serious car crash, the host left Charles Laughton to introduce television history.

Source: [The Ed Sullivan Show official archive](https://www.edsullivan.com/timeline/elvis-presleys-first-appearance/)

## 20 · Saturday Toons

**They kept Popeye's entire village.**

The village built for Robin Williams's Popeye movie was not demolished. Its colorful wooden houses survived in Malta and became a tourist attraction.

Source: [King Features / Popeye history](https://popeye.com/history/)

## 21 · Cartoon Vault

**Those Earrings Used to Be Ears**

Betty Boop began as a dog-like character. As the studio made her human, her floppy canine ears evolved into the hoop earrings everyone recognizes.

Source: [Fleischer Studios](https://fleischerstudios.com/women/)

## 22 · Scooby & Friends

**The Dog Borrowed Sinatra’s Scat**

Scooby-Doo's name traces to Frank Sinatra's scat singing at the end of Strangers in the Night. A crooner helped name television's famously frightened dog.

Source: [United States Postal Service](https://news.usps.com/2018/07/10/thats-a-fact/)

## 23 · Toon Toys

**Optimus Borrowed a Marine’s Voice**

Peter Cullen based Optimus Prime on his older brother Larry, a Vietnam Marine veteran whose advice was to make heroism compassionate, rather than merely loud.

Source: [Hasbro / Peter Cullen tribute](https://hasbropr.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/remembering-peter-cullen)

## 24 · '90s Kids

**The Sponge Began in a Classroom**

SpongeBob's ancestor was Bob the Sponge, created while Stephen Hillenburg taught marine biology at an ocean institute. The cartoon's creator really did teach sea life.

Source: [Nickelodeon Animation](https://www.nickanimation.com/creators/)

## 25 · After School

**An Ancient Gadget for Mars**

Bill Nye spotted a camera-calibration post and proposed turning it into a sundial for Mars, giving a spacecraft instrument an unexpectedly ancient second job.

Source: [NASA Astronomy Picture of the Day](https://apod.nasa.gov/apod/ap990428.html)

## 26 · Adult Swim

**Master Shake’s Audition Got Deleted**

After Dave Willis erased Dana Snyder's voicemail audition, Snyder returned to the same restaurant, repeated his drinks, and recorded it again at three in the morning.

Source: [Dave Willis interview / Inverse oral history](https://www.inverse.com/entertainment/adult-swim-20-years-anniversary)

## 27 · Puppet Show

**Cookie Monster Once Had Teeth**

Cookie Monster's ancestor, the Wheel Stealer, began in a snack commercial with teeth. Before Sesame Street, he also appeared for IBM and on Ed Sullivan.

Source: [Smithsonian National Museum of American History](https://www.si.edu/object/nmah_1449002)

## 28 · Caped Crusaders

**Three Weeks to Build the Batmobile**

The 1966 Batmobile started as a futuristic Lincoln show car. George Barris's team had just three weeks to turn the Futura into Batman's ride.

Source: [Barris Kustom / original builder](https://barris.com/cars/tv-movie-cars/batmobile/)

## 30 · Drive-In

**The rain was a sprinkler.**

Richard Hollingshead tested drive-in movies with a projector on his car hood, a bedsheet between trees, and lawn sprinklers standing in for bad weather.

Source: [Drive-Ins.com Museum](https://www.drive-ins.com/museum/richard-hollingshead-story)

## 31 · After Dark

**Noir, with a side of sausage.**

The Third Man's haunting zither music came from Anton Karas, whom director Carol Reed discovered playing in a tiny beer-and-sausage restaurant.

Source: [The Criterion Collection](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/25-the-third-man)

## 32 · Creature Feature

**The zombies ate ham.**

Night of the Living Dead's gruesome menu was surprisingly ordinary: Bosco chocolate syrup supplied the blood, while a local butcher donated roasted ham for the flesh.

Source: [Carnegie Museums](https://carnegiemuseums.org/carnegie-magazine/fall-2021/five-things-fall-2021/)

## 33 · Chiller Hosts

**Elvis helped create Elvira.**

Before Elvira, Cassandra Peterson was a Vegas showgirl. She credits Elvis Presley with urging her to leave and try singing, helping redirect her career.

Source: [Cassandra Peterson / Associated Press interview](https://apnews.com/article/cd20ece22e6b17efc7d449841927adb1)

## 34 · Silent Screen

**Stand here. Do not flinch.**

For Steamboat Bill, Jr., Buster Keaton stood beneath a real falling house front. He said its window cleared each shoulder by just two inches.

Source: [Buster Keaton interview / The New Yorker](https://www.newyorker.com/culture/richard-brody/dvd-of-the-week-steamboat-bill-jr)

## 35 · Laugh Track

**Eight fingers. One famous clock.**

Harold Lloyd performed Safety Last!'s dizzying climbing stunts after losing his right thumb and index finger. A specially made glove concealed the missing digits.

Source: [The Criterion Collection](https://www.criterion.com/current/posts/2804-safety-last-high-flying-harold)

## 36 · Kung Fu Theater

**Spot the future action legends.**

Enter the Dragon opens with Bruce Lee sparring against Sammo Hung. Another future superstar appears among the fighters later: a still-unknown Jackie Chan.

Source: [Turner Classic Movies](https://www.tcm.com/articles/25857/enter-the-dragon)

## 37 · Frontier

**That tumbleweed is an immigrant.**

The classic Western tumbleweed, Russian thistle, reached the United States in contaminated flax seed from Russia. Its American invasion began in South Dakota in 1873.

Source: [University of California IPM](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/russian-thistle/)

## 39 · Private Eye

**The hat was the illustrator's idea.**

Conan Doyle never specifically called Sherlock Holmes's hat a deerstalker. Illustrator Sidney Paget supplied the detective's famous look in drawings accompanying the original stories.

Source: [Conan Doyle Collection](https://www.conandoylecollection.com/conan-doyle/blog/read/2025/04/when-sherlock-got-his-deerstalker-b143)

## 40 · Christmas Movies

**The snow was too crunchy.**

Movie snow once used white-painted cornflakes. For It's a Wonderful Life, effects artists developed quieter foam-based snow so footsteps would not drown out the dialogue.

Source: [LIFE photo archive](https://time.com/3613817/its-a-wonderful-life-rare-photos-from-the-set-of-a-holiday-classic/)

## 41 · Showstoppers

**It almost faced the other way.**

West Side Story began as East Side Story: a Romeo-and-Juliet musical about Catholic and Jewish families on Manhattan's Lower East Side, before the concept changed.

Source: [Leonard Bernstein Office](https://leonardbernstein.com/works/view/9/west-side-story)

## 50 · Anime Station

**They mixed fifty new colors.**

Akira's animators did not settle for an ordinary paint box. Its 327-color palette included fifty colors mixed specifically for that single film's production.

Source: [BFI / Sight and Sound](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/lists/50-key-anime-films)

## 51 · Dubbed Anime

**Three shows wearing one trench coat.**

Robotech was assembled from three separate Japanese series: Macross, Southern Cross and Mospeada. New scripts turned their unrelated stories into one continuing American saga.

Source: [Carl Macek's Robotech production notes](https://copland.udel.edu/~mm/anime/robotech.html)

## 52 · Retro Anime

**Cartoon characters had acting careers.**

Astro Boy creator Osamu Tezuka treated his recurring characters as actors, casting familiar faces in different stories and even imagining how much they were paid.

Source: [Tezuka Osamu Official](https://tezukaosamu.net/en/character/star_system.html)

## 53 · AMV Hour

**An album became an anime.**

Daft Punk's Discovery became Interstella 5555, a feature-length anime adventure with no dialogue. The songs and animation tell the story of an abducted alien band.

Source: [Batalha Centro de Cinema](https://www.batalhacentrodecinema.pt/en/program/interstella-5555-the-5tory-of-the-5ecret-5tar-5ystem/)

## 54 · Kaiju Theater

**Godzilla is secretly a bass player.**

Godzilla's original roar was not an animal recording. Composer Akira Ifukube dragged a resin-coated leather glove along a double bass's loosened strings to create it.

Source: [Godzilla sound designers / NPR](https://www.wbur.org/npr/312839612/whats-in-a-roar-crafting-godzillas-iconic-sound?f=312839612&ft=3)

## 55 · Mecha Hangar

**The toy company picked the paint.**

Gundam's bright colors were not purely a creative choice. Creator Yoshiyuki Tomino said the toy-company sponsor required the robot to use red, white and blue.

Source: [Yoshiyuki Tomino / Anime NYC interview](https://www.animeherald.com/interview/anime-nyc-2019-yoshiyuki-tomino-talks-to-anime-herald/)

## 56 · Magical Girls

**Venus got there before the Moon.**

Sailor Venus was a solo heroine before Sailor Moon existed. Naoko Takeuchi first gave Minako her own adventures as Sailor V, guided by Artemis.

Source: [Kodansha](https://kodansha.us/2020/03/18/summer-fall-2020-debuting-print-titles-revealed/)

## 57 · Power Level

**His wife named the attack.**

Akira Toriyama struggled to name Master Roshi's signature attack. His wife suggested Kamehameha; he liked how silly and appropriate it sounded, and kept it.

Source: [Akira Toriyama interview / Ian Kelley translation](https://tsoj.manga.org/db/tori_4.html)

## 58 · OVA Vault

**Chicago was not just a backdrop.**

Gunsmith Cats' animators traveled to Chicago to research the real thing, visiting gun shops, police facilities and the Police Academy instead of inventing every detail.

Source: [Fred Patten / Cartoon Research](https://cartoonresearch.com/index.php/forgotten-ovas-11-gunsmith-cats/)

## 59 · Toonami Tapes

**TOM has a space-cowboy voice.**

Toonami's robot host TOM shares his voice with Cowboy Bebop's Spike Spiegel. Both are performed by Steve Blum, who also voices several versions of Wolverine.

Source: [Steve Blum official biography](https://www.steveblumvoices.com/about)

## 60 · Gridiron

**Sorry, coach has another game.**

Portsmouth's star Dutch Clark missed the NFL's first playoff. He had already returned to his other job: coaching basketball at Colorado College.

Source: [Pro Football Hall of Fame](https://www.profootballhof.com/football-history/the-first-playoff-game)

## 61 · Rink Side

**Eight arms, eight wins.**

Detroit’s octopus tradition began with seafood-shop brothers in 1952. Its eight arms represented the eight playoff victories then needed to win the Stanley Cup.

Source: [Canadian Museum of History](https://www.historymuseum.ca/blog/fan-rituals-a-part-of-hockey-history)

## 62 · Ringside

**The champ also fixed things.**

Heavyweight champion Jack Johnson was also an inventor. He patented an improved wrench in 1922, giving him another way to deliver a powerful grip.

Source: [Smithsonian Folklife](https://folklife.si.edu/magazine/jim-crow-museum-racist-objects-social-justice)

## 63 · Pit Lane

**Blame Dan for the champagne.**

After winning Le Mans in 1967, Dan Gurney shook his champagne and sprayed the people nearby. That spontaneous celebration became a motorsport podium ritual.

Source: [24 Hours of Le Mans](https://www.24h-lemans.com/en/news/24-hours-stories-dan-gurney-starts-podium-champagne-spray-tradition-55835)

## 64 · Skate & Surf

**The wetsuit had nuclear roots.**

Neoprene wetsuit pioneer Hugh Bradner was a Manhattan Project physicist. He began improving diving gear as a weekend project while working at Berkeley.

Source: [Scripps Oceanography](https://scripps.ucsd.edu/news/obituary-notice-renowned-physicist-and-inventor-wetsuit-hugh-bradner)

## 65 · Sweatin'

**The workout sold the machine.**

Jane Fonda says her Workout helped sell VCRs: people finally had a tape they wanted to play repeatedly, rather than watch just once.

Source: [Jane Fonda](https://janefonda.com/2010/01/excited/)

## 66 · Hoops

**No instant replay.**

There is no available video footage of Wilt Chamberlain’s 100-point game. The NBA’s record scoring night left fans without a replay.

Source: [NBA / New Orleans Pelicans](https://www.nba.com/pelicans/news/many-ways-1970s-nba-new-orleans-jazz-bears-no-resemblance-todays-league)

## 67 · Ballpark

**Disco stopped baseball.**

Chicago’s Disco Demolition Night blew up records between games. Fans invaded the field, and the White Sox forfeited game two.

Source: [MLB / Chicago White Sox](https://www.mlb.com/whitesox/news/disco-demolition-night-chicago-comiskey-park)

## 70 · Video Jukebox

**This trophy actually went to space.**

MTV’s Moonman wasn’t only pretending to be an astronaut. A Video Music Award statue flew aboard the Russian space station Mir in 1996.

Source: [National Air and Space Museum](https://airandspace.si.edu/stories/editorial/meet-moonman)

## 71 · Soul Train

**Four hundred dollars started the party.**

Don Cornelius made the Soul Train pilot with $400 of his own money. Its name came from a road show he created for local high schools.

Source: [Smithsonian Archives Center](https://sirismm.si.edu/EADpdfs/NMAH.AC.1223.pdf)

## 72 · Headbangers

**Heavy metal needed washing-up bottles.**

After losing fingertips in a factory accident, Tony Iommi melted plastic washing-up bottles into replacement fingertips so he could keep playing guitar.

Source: [Tony Iommi official biography](https://www.iommi.com/biography/)

## 73 · Jazz Club

**The bent trumpet was an accident.**

Dizzy Gillespie’s trumpet was accidentally bent during a party. He liked its strange new sound and had later trumpets deliberately built with the same bend.

Source: [National Museum of American History](https://www.americanhistory.si.edu/explore/exhibitions/entertainment-nation/online/dizzy-gillespies-trumpet)

## 74 · Encore

**Woodstock’s finale came after breakfast.**

Jimi Hendrix was supposed to close Woodstock around midnight. Delays pushed his performance to Monday morning, when roughly 40,000 people remained from the enormous crowd.

Source: [Bethel Woods Museum](https://www.bethelwoodscenter.org/news/detail/jimi-hendrix-50-years-of-peace-music)

## 80 · Ephemera

**The cardboard caught nuclear fallout.**

Kodak traced mysterious film spots to radioactive cardboard. Trinity-test fallout had contaminated the river water used by the Indiana mill making its packaging.

Source: [ORAU Radiation Museum](https://www.orau.org/health-physics-museum/collection/nuclear-weapons/trinity/kodak-film.html)

## 81 · Duck & Cover

**Vegas partied under mushroom clouds.**

Las Vegas hotels promoted views of nuclear tests. Casinos hosted dawn parties, served atomic cocktails, and advertised detonation times as a tourist attraction.

Source: [Atomic Heritage Foundation](https://ahf.nuclearmuseum.org/ahf/location/nevada-test-site/)

## 82 · Home Movies

**Your movie took two laps.**

Regular 8mm film started twice as wide. You filmed one side, flipped the spool, filmed the other, then the lab slit it lengthwise and joined the ends.

Source: [Chicago Film Archives](https://www.chicagofilmarchives.org/news/2017/12/scanning-joann-elams-filmabuse-original/)

## 83 · Computer Chronicles

**The bug is still in the book.**

In 1947, Harvard engineers found a moth in their computer and taped it into the logbook. That literal bug is now in the Smithsonian collection.

Source: [National Museum of American History](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_334663)

## 84 · Mission Control

**The Moon landing survived a reboot.**

Apollo 11’s overloaded guidance computer repeatedly restarted during descent. It preserved essential landing tasks while dropping unnecessary radar jobs, letting the astronauts continue toward the surface.

Source: [NASA Lunar Surface Journal](https://history.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a11/a11.1201-pa.html)

## 85 · Wild Kingdom

**Nature invented the poop cube.**

Wombats produce cube-shaped poop inside their intestines. Researchers found that alternating stiff and stretchy regions help sculpt the corners before anything leaves the animal.

Source: [Georgia Tech / Soft Matter](https://hu.gatech.edu/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/Hu21-wombat.pdf)

## 86 · TV Dinner

**Before soufflés, secret files.**

Before television, Julia Child worked for the OSS. One assignment placed her with a rescue-equipment unit developing shark repellent, long before her famous French recipes.

Source: [CIA History](https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/julia-child-and-the-oss-recipe-for-shark-repellent/)

## 87 · Strange Signals

**Area 51 took off accidentally.**

The first U-2 became airborne at Area 51 during a high-speed taxi test. Its pilot had intended to stay on the ground that day.

Source: [CIA History](https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/area-51-and-the-accidental-test-flight)

## 88 · Slow TV

**The TV fire burned something.**

The original televised Yule Log was filmed at New York’s Gracie Mansion. A stray spark damaged an antique rug after the crew removed the protective screen.

Source: [CultureNOW Museum Without Walls](https://culturenow.org/site/3cca5924-4446-4ece-876d-5f475c84b302)

## 89 · Arcade

**Too popular to keep working.**

Pong’s bar prototype was reported broken after two weeks. The fault wasn’t electronics: its milk-carton coin catcher had become jammed with quarters.

Source: [Computer History Museum](https://www.computerhistory.org/revolution/story/183)

## 90 · Starbase

**The Enterprise had a bad side.**

The original Enterprise filming model had exposed wiring on one side. Its glamorous screen appearance hid a much rougher backstage side.

Source: [Smithsonian](https://www.startrek.com/news/enterprise-studio-model-back-on-display)

## 91 · Hyperdrive

**Vader's lungs came from a dive shop.**

Darth Vader's breathing began with sound designer Ben Burtt breathing through a scuba regulator. He put the microphone inside the regulator to capture it.

Source: [Lucasfilm / StarWars.com](https://www.starwars.com/news/5-iconic-star-wars-sound-effects-and-how-they-were-made-starwars-com)

## 92 · Riff Raff

**Crow was partly a bowling pin.**

Crow T. Robot's homemade anatomy includes a plastic bowling pin, a lacrosse mask and Tupperware. The wisecracking robot really was assembled from ordinary stuff.

Source: [Joel Hodgson / Team MST3K](https://mst3k.backerkit.com/hosted_preorders/project_updates?page=9)

## 93 · Atomic Age

**This toy came with actual uranium.**

The Gilbert U-238 Atomic Energy Lab gave children uranium ore, radioactive sources and a Geiger counter. It was sold as an educational toy in 1950-51.

Source: [ORAU Museum of Radiation](https://www.orau.org/health-physics-museum/collection/toys/gilbert-u-238-atomic-energy-lab.html)

## 94 · Deep Space

**We sent someone's brainwaves into space.**

Voyager's Golden Record carries music, greetings and a recording of Ann Druyan's brainwaves. Humanity's message to the stars includes the electrical activity of a human mind.

Source: [Library of Congress](https://www.loc.gov/static/programs/national-recording-preservation-board/documents/Voyager-MurmursoftheEarth.pdf)

## 95 · World of Tomorrow

**EPCOT was supposed to have residents.**

Walt Disney's original EPCOT was a working city where people would actually live. The theme park came later; the city of tomorrow was never built.

Source: [Disney / D23](https://d23.com/a-world-of-tomorrow-inside-walts-last-dream/)

## 96 · Cliffhangers

**Takeoff was hand-drawn.**

The 1948 live-action Superman serial switched to a cartoon Superman for flying effects. An actor could play the Man of Steel; drawings handled the airborne work.

Source: [Turner Classic Movies](https://www.tcm.com/articles/149238/superman-1948)

## 97 · Subspace 97

**Uhura's fan changed her mind.**

Nichelle Nichols planned to leave Star Trek. Then Martin Luther King Jr. told her how much Uhura mattered, and she decided to stay.

Source: [PBS / Nichelle Nichols interview](https://www.pbs.org/wnet/pioneers-of-television/pioneering-people/nichelle-nichols/)
