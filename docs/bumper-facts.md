# Behind the bumpers

The library contains six station IDs and six researched stories for each of 76 channels, plus 36 additional Subspace stories: 948 videos, including 492 distinct fact/story cards. Copy is paraphrased for a short reading window. Sources are linked with each story.

## 2 · Ad Break

### 1. Eighty-four miles of bunny thread.

Energizer's giant Bunny balloon needed 84 miles of thread and size-98 feet. The battery-ad mascot became an airborne craft project on an absurd scale.

Source: [Energizer](https://energizer.com/energizer-bunny/bunny-timeline/)

### 2. A Jingle Delayed by Fog

A fog-diverted flight helped inspire Coca-Cola’s Hilltop commercial. Watching frustrated passengers relax together over drinks gave advertising executive Bill Backer an idea worth singing.

Source: [The Coca-Cola Company / advertising history](https://www.coca-colacompany.com/about-us/history/id-like-to-buy-the-world-a-coke)

### 3. Eighteen Bottles for One Smile

Joe Greene estimated he drank eighteen sixteen-ounce Cokes while filming his famous jersey commercial. Getting that heartwarming football moment required a surprisingly enormous soda supply.

Source: [Yahoo Sports / Joe Greene interview](https://sports.yahoo.com/iconic-sports-commercials-mean-joe-greenes-hey-kid-catch-055944330.html)

### 4. Beef Ended Everything

Wendy’s dropped Clara Peller after a spaghetti-sauce commercial implied she had found the beef. A rival product answered her famous question.

Source: [Los Angeles Times / 1985 reporting](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1985-03-23-fi-21253-story.html)

### 5. A Labrador Became a Polar Bear

Coca-Cola’s animated polar bears owe something to a Labrador puppy. Creator Ken Stewart thought his fluffy dog resembled a polar bear, helping spark the campaign.

Source: [The Coca-Cola Company / Ken Stewart](https://www.coca-colacompany.com/about-us/history/coca-colas-polar-bears)

### 6. The Name Nobody Could Pronounce

GEICO’s gecko grew out of a pronunciation problem: people confused the company’s name with the lizard. The advertising team turned that misunderstanding into a mascot.

Source: [NPR / advertising interview](https://www.wvia.org/business/2006-08-19/geico-ads-go-beyond-the-gecko)

## 3 · Rerun Alley

### 1. Four Minutes, One Famous Fall

Dick Van Dyke's famous ottoman opening was filmed in about four minutes. Carl Reiner requested a second version where he dodged it, keeping viewers guessing.

Source: [PBS American Masters / John Rich interview](https://video.wttw.com/video/what-made-the-opening-credits-of-the-dick-van-dyke-show-special-vvlegk/)

### 2. Homework Stranded These Castaways

Gilligan’s Island began with a college speaking assignment. Asked what he would bring to a desert island, Sherwood Schwartz kept thinking about the question for years.

Source: [Mental Floss / Gilligan production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/15-fateful-facts-about-gilligans-island)

### 3. Gilligan Came From the Phone Book

Sherwood Schwartz found Gilligan’s name in a telephone directory. He and Bob Denver later disagreed about whether Gilligan was the character’s first or last name.

Source: [Mental Floss / Gilligan production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/15-fateful-facts-about-gilligans-island)

### 4. The Skipper Arrived on Horseback

Alan Hale left a Utah western location to audition for Gilligan’s Island, traveling by horse, hitchhiking, plane and taxi before returning for the next day’s filming.

Source: [Mental Floss / Gilligan production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/15-fateful-facts-about-gilligans-island)

### 5. A National Tragedy in the Credits

Gilligan’s Island’s opening harbor footage contains a flag at half-staff. Production in Honolulu coincided with the aftermath of President Kennedy’s assassination, leaving history inside the credits.

Source: [Mental Floss / Gilligan production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/15-fateful-facts-about-gilligans-island)

### 6. He Chose the Talking Car

Jerry Van Dyke turned down Gilligan’s Island. He instead starred in My Mother the Car, leaving Bob Denver to become television’s most recognizable hapless castaway.

Source: [Mental Floss / Gilligan production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/15-fateful-facts-about-gilligans-island)

## 4 · Late Night

### 1. The Pencil That Could Only Erase

Johnny Carson had pencils specially made with erasers on both ends for The Tonight Show. Even his desk stationery had its own comedy routine.

Source: [Museum of Broadcast Communications](https://www.museum.tv/gift-shop/p/carson-pencil)

### 2. Conan Built Springfield’s Monorail

Before hosting late-night television, Conan O’Brien wrote The Simpsons’ monorail episode. Its runaway civic disaster mixed a Music Man-style salesman with big disaster-movie spectacle.

Source: [Conan O’Brien / The Howard Stern Show](https://www.howardstern.com/news/2022/11/09/conan-obrien-talks-comedy-highs-and-late-night-lows-in-stern-show-return/)

### 3. The Guest Brought His Own Lever

Conan’s giant lever played absurd Walker, Texas Ranger clips. Chuck Norris eventually visited with his own lever, triggering footage of himself kicking Conan into cardboard boxes.

Source: [The Atlantic / Chuck Norris retrospective](https://www.theatlantic.com/culture/2026/03/chuck-norris-appreciation/686494/)

### 4. The Copyboy Crossed the Street

Dick Cavett was a TIME copyboy before becoming a talk-show host. He carried jokes across the street to Jack Paar, who hired him as a writer.

Source: [TIME / Dick Cavett interview](https://time.com/3580806/dick-cavett-video/)

### 5. A Bathroom Joke Emptied the Desk

Jack Paar walked off The Tonight Show after NBC removed a water-closet joke. Announcer Hugh Downs suddenly had to take over the broadcast without its host.

Source: [TIME / contemporary 1960 reporting](https://time.com/archive/6872710/television-after-appomattox/)

### 6. The Tomahawk Missed the Lesson

Ed Ames tried teaching Johnny Carson to throw a tomahawk. His demonstration struck a cardboard cowboy in the groin, turning an instructional segment into sustained audience laughter.

Source: [TV Guide / Johnny Carson retrospective](https://www.tvguide.com/news/johnny-carson-moments-1024634/)

## 5 · Big Prize

### 1. The Answers Came First

On a plane, Julann Griffin proposed giving contestants the answers. Her husband Merv tested it with 5,280; the question was feet in a mile.

Source: [Television Academy / Tony Griffin interview](https://www.televisionacademy.com/features/news/hall-fame/merv-griffin-hall-fame-tribute)

### 2. The Buzzer Once Interrupted Alex

Early Jeopardy! contestants could buzz before Alex Trebek finished reading. The show changed the rules in its second season, making players wait for the complete clue.

Source: [Jeopardy! / official production history](https://www.jeopardy.com/jbuzz/behind-scenes/when-jeopardy-buzzer-timing-first-became-thing)

### 3. Even Alex’s Mother Complained

Jeopardy! briefly moved its returning champion to another lectern. Complaints helped reverse the experiment after four shows; even Alex Trebek’s mother objected to the new arrangement.

Source: [Jeopardy! / official production history](https://www.jeopardy.com/jbuzz/behind-scenes/when-jeopardy-buzzer-timing-first-became-thing)

### 4. Walter White Missed the Showcase

Before Breaking Bad, Aaron Paul appeared as an exuberant contestant on The Price Is Right. He reached the showcase round but went over the price.

Source: [Mental Floss / The Price Is Right history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/game-shows/14-showcased-facts-about-price-right)

### 5. Come On Down Takes an Interview

The Price Is Right does not simply pick contestants at random. Audience members are interviewed before taping, with producers looking for enthusiasm, personality and potential television chemistry.

Source: [Mental Floss / The Price Is Right history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/game-shows/14-showcased-facts-about-price-right)

### 6. Easy Games Had an Expensive Result

Producer Roger Dobkowitz selected easier games to help Drew Carey settle into The Price Is Right. The generous stretch left the show roughly $700,000 over budget.

Source: [Mental Floss / The Price Is Right history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/game-shows/14-showcased-facts-about-price-right)

## 6 · Brit TV

### 1. Basil Was Someone’s Actual Boss

John Cleese based Basil Fawlty on real hotelier Donald Sinclair, whose behavior included correcting guests' table manners. A disastrous hotel stay became a classic sitcom.

Source: [RTÉ / John Cleese](https://www.rte.ie/entertainment/2003/1008/400206-fawltytowers/)

### 2. The BBC Had Already Printed It

The Python team could not simply discard Flying Circus: the BBC had already printed the title. Monty Python emerged as the extra name attached to it.

Source: [Mental Floss / Monty Python production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/movies/15-facts-about-monty-python-and-holy-grail)

### 3. A Moose Invaded the Credits

Holy Grail’s opening credits pretend to be hijacked by Swedish subtitles and moose jokes. The deliberately broken introduction spoofs the seriousness of prestigious foreign cinema.

Source: [Mental Floss / Monty Python production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/movies/15-facts-about-monty-python-and-holy-grail)

### 4. Rock Stars Bankrolled the Knights

Monty Python and the Holy Grail found unlikely financial knights: Pink Floyd, Led Zeppelin and Genesis helped bankroll the film alongside its other early investors.

Source: [Mental Floss / Monty Python production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/movies/15-facts-about-monty-python-and-holy-grail)

### 5. Epic Books on a Living-Room Floor

Holy Grail’s illuminated manuscript inserts were filmed on Terry Gilliam’s living-room floor. His wife, Maggie Weston, turned the pages for the supposedly grand medieval presentation.

Source: [Mental Floss / Monty Python production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/movies/15-facts-about-monty-python-and-holy-grail)

### 6. The Army Earned Two Pounds

Holy Grail’s imposing final army included 175 Stirling students. Their payment was two pounds each, plus food and transportation: a medieval force recruited on a student budget.

Source: [Mental Floss / Monty Python production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/movies/15-facts-about-monty-python-and-holy-grail)

## 7 · PBS

### 1. His Mother Was the Wardrobe Department

The Smithsonian's Mister Rogers cardigan still carries his mother's name inside: Nancy knitted it herself, just as she made sweaters he wore on television.

Source: [Smithsonian National Museum of American History](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_680637)

### 2. A Viewer Changed the Fish Routine

A blind young viewer worried that Mister Rogers never fed his fish. He began explaining the feeding aloud, making the familiar routine reassuring for children who could not see.

Source: [Fred Rogers Productions / The Television House](https://www.misterrogers.org/articles/the-television-house/)

### 3. A Missing Bathroom Needed Explaining

A child thought Mister Rogers never used a bathroom because his television house had none visible. Rogers answered the concern by showing viewers the bathroom.

Source: [Fred Rogers Productions / The Television House](https://www.misterrogers.org/articles/the-television-house/)

### 4. Trolley Had an Ordinary Switch

Mister Rogers showed children the switch that controlled Trolley. Rather than protecting the illusion, he let viewers see how a familiar piece of television actually worked.

Source: [Fred Rogers Productions / The Television House](https://www.misterrogers.org/articles/the-television-house/)

### 5. Sneakers Started Behind the Scenes

Mister Rogers’ sneakers had a practical backstage origin. On The Children’s Corner, quiet footwear helped him move between the puppet area and the organ without making noise.

Source: [Around the Neighborhood / original 1973 newsletter](https://neighborhoodarchive.com/publications/newsletters/atn0103/index.html)

### 6. Julia Child’s Other Recipe Department

Before cooking television, Julia Child worked for the OSS alongside officers developing shark repellent to prevent curious sharks from setting off explosives intended for submarines.

Source: [CIA / Julia Child’s OSS service](https://www.cia.gov/stories/story/julia-child-cooking-up-spy-ops-for-oss/)

## 8 · Y2K WB

### 1. All That Talking Filled Extra Pages

Gilmore Girls burned through 77–78-page scripts; its showrunner said the crew shot nine or ten pages daily to keep up with all that conversation.

Source: [David S. Rosenthal interview / BuddyTV](https://www.buddytv.com/exclusive-interview-david-s-rosenthal-head-writer-and-executive-producer-on-gilmore-girls/)

### 2. Stars Hollow Began With Self-Service Coffee

An inn visit in Washington, Connecticut helped inspire Stars Hollow. Amy Sherman-Palladino noticed the small-town atmosphere, including a diner customer casually serving himself coffee.

Source: [Mental Floss / Gilmore Girls production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/22-things-you-might-not-know-about-gilmore-girls)

### 3. Sookie Almost Had Another Face

Alex Borstein originally played Sookie in the Gilmore Girls pilot. Mad TV commitments prevented her staying, but she later returned as Drella and Miss Celine.

Source: [Mental Floss / Gilmore Girls production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/22-things-you-might-not-know-about-gilmore-girls)

### 4. A Failed Rory Audition Created Paris

Liza Weil auditioned to play Rory on Gilmore Girls. The creators chose another actress, then wrote the formidable Paris Geller specifically to bring Weil into the show.

Source: [Mental Floss / Gilmore Girls production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/22-things-you-might-not-know-about-gilmore-girls)

### 5. A Thousand Daisies Looked Too Small

Gilmore Girls’ thousand-yellow-daisies proposal needed far more than a thousand flowers. The crew repeatedly ordered more because the stated number barely filled the intended space.

Source: [Mental Floss / Gilmore Girls production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/22-things-you-might-not-know-about-gilmore-girls)

### 6. Jess’s California Show Cost Too Much

Jess almost headlined a Gilmore Girls spinoff set in Venice Beach. The seaside location proved too expensive, and the proposed series never moved beyond its setup episode.

Source: [Mental Floss / Gilmore Girls production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/22-things-you-might-not-know-about-gilmore-girls)

## 9 · Porchlight

### 1. The Moose Had a University Job

The moose strolling through Northern Exposure's opening credits was Morty, a Washington State University research animal with a second career in television.

Source: [Washington State University](https://timeline.wsu.edu/timeline/marty-the-moose/)

### 2. Marilyn Refused the Stereotype

Elaine Miles challenged Northern Exposure’s original ideas for Marilyn’s braids and slow speech. Her objections helped replace stereotyped choices with a more natural performance and hairstyle.

### 4. Cicely Left a Festival Behind

Northern Exposure’s Alaska was largely Roslyn, Washington. Its fictional town developed such a devoted following that fans gathered there for a festival called Moosefest.

### 5. The Weird Episode Nearly Stayed Shelved

CBS executives worried Northern Exposure’s dreamlike Aurora Borealis episode was too strange. Its successful reception helped give the writers more freedom to experiment afterward.

### 6. An Audition Became an Awkward Introduction

After auditioning, Rob Morrow told Janine Turner he thought it would be the two of them. He meant the casting; she initially heard a pickup line.

## 10 · Prime Time Tapes

### 1. A Fake Ending Guarded the Real One

To protect Newhart's surprise finale, a fake ending sent his character to heaven. Suzanne Pleshette stayed hidden until the secret final scene was filmed.

Source: [Television Academy Interviews](https://interviews.televisionacademy.com/shows/newhart-the-last-newhart)

### 2. Their Time Capsule Lasted Two Months

M*A*S*H buried a time capsule near the end of production. A construction worker uncovered it roughly two months later, dramatically shortening its intended journey into the future.

Source: [Mental Floss / M*A*S*H production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/17-painless-facts-about-mash)

### 3. Radar’s Bear Came Back at Auction

Radar’s teddy bear vanished for decades after M*A*S*H. It resurfaced at auction, where a medical student bought it before eventually selling it back to Gary Burghoff.

Source: [Mental Floss / M*A*S*H production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/17-painless-facts-about-mash)

### 4. No Canned Laughs During Surgery

M*A*S*H’s producers resisted a laugh track. Their compromise kept canned laughter out of operating-room scenes; British broadcasts went further and removed the track entirely.

Source: [Mental Floss / M*A*S*H production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/17-painless-facts-about-mash)

### 5. The Patients Had Baseball Names

M*A*S*H writers sometimes borrowed patient names from baseball rosters. Members of the California Angels and Los Angeles Dodgers quietly supplied identities for fictional Korean War casualties.

Source: [Mental Floss / M*A*S*H production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/17-painless-facts-about-mash)

### 6. The Contract Was Never Signed

When Wayne Rogers left M*A*S*H, a threatened contract dispute hit an unusual obstacle: he had never signed his contract, having objected to its proposed terms.

Source: [Mental Floss / M*A*S*H production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/17-painless-facts-about-mash)

## 11 · Strange Tales

### 1. The Theme Was a Splice

The Twilight Zone's famous theme joined two separate pieces from CBS's music library. Marius Constant had written them as short fragments, never as that signature tune.

Source: [Rod Serling Memorial Foundation](https://rodserling.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Serling_Newsletter_Spring-2023-Final.pdf)

### 2. The Dimension That Went Missing

Rod Serling initially called The Twilight Zone a sixth dimension. An executive asked what happened to the fifth, prompting the familiar introduction to be recorded again.

Source: [Mental Floss / Rod Serling and The Twilight Zone](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/8-bizarre-facts-about-rod-serling-and-twilight-zone)

### 3. An Oscar Winner Filled the Budget Gap

The Twilight Zone saved money by buying the French short An Occurrence at Owl Creek Bridge. An already Oscar-winning film became an episode instead of another costly production.

Source: [Mental Floss / Rod Serling and The Twilight Zone](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/8-bizarre-facts-about-rod-serling-and-twilight-zone)

### 4. The Zone Tried Canned Laughter

Carol Burnett’s Twilight Zone episode originally used a laugh track. The comedy was considered a possible spinoff launch; later reruns removed the canned audience response.

Source: [Mental Floss / Rod Serling and The Twilight Zone](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/8-bizarre-facts-about-rod-serling-and-twilight-zone)

### 5. Only Serling Could Say That Word

Writer Richard Matheson recalled that other Twilight Zone writers had God crossed out of scripts, while Rod Serling could use the word. The unexplained exception puzzled him.

Source: [Mental Floss / Rod Serling and The Twilight Zone](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/8-bizarre-facts-about-rod-serling-and-twilight-zone)

### 6. Fourteen Thousand Unwanted Trips to the Zone

The Twilight Zone received about 14,000 unsolicited scripts. The enormous pile produced little usable material, and the series relied instead on established science-fiction writers.

Source: [Mental Floss / Rod Serling and The Twilight Zone](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/8-bizarre-facts-about-rod-serling-and-twilight-zone)

## 12 · Sitcom Gold

### 1. Two Babies, One Broadcast Day

Lucy Ricardo's baby arrived on television the same day Lucille Ball delivered Desi Jr. Her real birth was coordinated with the broadcast through a planned cesarean.

Source: [Television Academy / Desi Arnaz Jr. interview](https://www.televisionacademy.com/features/news/online-originals/heres-50-years-heres-lucy-part-1)

### 2. Lucy Insisted Ricky Be Desi

Lucille Ball would only make I Love Lucy with Desi Arnaz as her husband. Executives doubted audiences would believe the real married couple belonged together.

Source: [Lucille Ball Desi Arnaz Museum / Fast Facts](https://lucydesi.com/i-love-lucy-history/fast-facts/)

### 3. The Yankees Could Write Fred Out

William Frawley’s I Love Lucy contract let him miss work for Yankees World Series games. The production actually wrote Fred Mertz out of two episodes.

Source: [Lucille Ball Desi Arnaz Museum / Fast Facts](https://lucydesi.com/i-love-lucy-history/fast-facts/)

### 4. Lucy’s Mother Is in the Soundtrack

Lucille Ball’s mother attended every I Love Lucy taping. Her distinctive laugh survives in the soundtrack, making the studio audience a small family archive.

Source: [Lucille Ball Desi Arnaz Museum / Fast Facts](https://lucydesi.com/i-love-lucy-history/fast-facts/)

### 5. The Lost Pilot Was at a Friend’s

I Love Lucy’s pilot was considered lost for almost forty years. A surviving print finally turned up in the home of Desi Arnaz’s collaborator Pepito Perez.

Source: [Lucille Ball Desi Arnaz Museum / Fast Facts](https://lucydesi.com/i-love-lucy-history/fast-facts/)

### 6. The Famous Heart Was a Replacement

I Love Lucy originally opened with animated Lucy and Ricky characters created by Hanna-Barbera. The familiar satin heart replaced those cartoon credits for syndicated reruns.

Source: [Lucille Ball Desi Arnaz Museum / Fast Facts](https://lucydesi.com/i-love-lucy-history/fast-facts/)

## 13 · Spooky '90s

### 1. Scully Recruited Beyond the Screen

In a 2018 survey, half the women familiar with Dana Scully said the X-Files character increased their interest in science, technology, engineering or mathematics.

Source: [Geena Davis Institute / 2018 Scully Effect study](https://geenadavisinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/x-files-scully-effect-report-geena-davis-institute.pdf)

### 2. An Oscar Winner Played the Tattoo

The X-Files gave Jodie Foster an unusually small but sinister role: she voiced the talking tattoo in Never Again, turning body art into an unseen guest star.

Source: [Mental Floss / The X-Files production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/20-truths-about-x-files)

### 3. The Extra Became the Conspiracy

The X-Files’ Cigarette Smoking Man began as a silent background presence. William B. Davis’s apparently minor part expanded into one of the series’ central villains.

Source: [Mental Floss / The X-Files production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/20-truths-about-x-files)

### 4. The Skeptic Believed More Off Camera

The X-Files reversed its stars’ real attitudes: Gillian Anderson expressed more openness to paranormal possibilities, while David Duchovny was more skeptical than his believer character Mulder.

Source: [Mental Floss / The X-Files production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/20-truths-about-x-files)

### 5. Skinner’s Head Almost Cost the Role

Mitch Pileggi’s shaved head nearly worked against his X-Files audition. Chris Carter initially found his appearance too intense; repeated auditions eventually produced Assistant Director Skinner.

Source: [Mental Floss / The X-Files production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/20-truths-about-x-files)

### 6. Mulder’s Kidnapper Helped Create Walter White

Bryan Cranston’s performance in The X-Files episode Drive impressed writer Vince Gilligan. That guest role later helped persuade Gilligan that Cranston could carry Breaking Bad.

Source: [Mental Floss / The X-Files production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/entertainment/tv/20-truths-about-x-files)

## 14 · Order Now

### 1. Thirty Thousand Pounds of Turkey

Ron Popeil said developing his Olive Oil Fryer consumed 30,000 pounds of turkey; much of the testing food went to firefighters and homeless people.

Source: [Ron Popeil interview / University of Illinois Alumni Association](https://uiaa.org/2013/12/13/alumni-interview-ron-popeil/)

### 2. The Checkout Line Inspired the Pitch

Ron Popeil noticed Costco shoppers watching rotisserie chickens turn while waiting in line. Their fascination helped inspire the countertop rotisserie he later sold on television.

Source: [Ron Popeil / University of Illinois Alumni Association interview](https://uiaa.org/2013/12/13/alumni-interview-ron-popeil/)

### 3. The Fish Took the Fishing Gadget

Ron Popeil recalled hooking a powerful king salmon with a Pocket Fisherman. The fish yanked the compact device from his hands and swam away with it.

Source: [Ron Popeil / University of Illinois Alumni Association interview](https://uiaa.org/2013/12/13/alumni-interview-ron-popeil/)

### 4. The One-Way Mirror Hid an Audience

As a young Chicago demonstrator, Ron Popeil worked beneath a manager’s one-way mirror. The manager showed visitors the salesman downstairs who was earning more than he did.

Source: [Ron Popeil / University of Illinois Alumni Association interview](https://uiaa.org/2013/12/13/alumni-interview-ron-popeil/)

### 5. The Borrowed Paper Had Different Grades

Ron Popeil recalled borrowing Mel Korey’s college paper and receiving a lower grade than its author. The friend behind that awkward episode later became his business partner.

Source: [Ron Popeil / University of Illinois Alumni Association interview](https://uiaa.org/2013/12/13/alumni-interview-ron-popeil/)

### 6. Flavor Flav Wandered Into the Infomercial

Ron Popeil said Flavor Flav unexpectedly joined a fryer infomercial shoot and offered an enthusiastic testimonial. A chance celebrity encounter became part of the sales presentation.

Source: [Ron Popeil / University of Illinois Alumni Association interview](https://uiaa.org/2013/12/13/alumni-interview-ron-popeil/)

## 15 · Paint Along

### 1. The Perm He Couldn’t Escape

Bob Ross got a perm to save money on haircuts, then grew tired of it. By then, his curly-haired portrait had become the company logo.

Source: [NPR / Annette Kowalski interview](https://www.northcountrypublicradio.org/news/npr/490923502/the-real-bob-ross-meet-the-meticulous-artist-behind-those-happy-trees)

### 2. The Bathtub Had an Alligator

Bob Ross’s love of rescuing animals started young. As a child, he brought home an injured alligator and tried nursing it in the family bathtub.

Source: [Mental Floss / Bob Ross interviews and production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/art/artists/35-happy-little-facts-about-bob-ross)

### 3. His Early Canvases Were Gold Pans

Before television fame, Bob Ross sold Alaskan landscapes painted inside gold pans. The unusual souvenirs let customers carry both a painting and a piece of frontier imagery.

Source: [Mental Floss / Bob Ross interviews and production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/art/artists/35-happy-little-facts-about-bob-ross)

### 4. A Missing Finger Never Stopped Him

Bob Ross lost part of his left index finger in a childhood woodworking accident. The injury did not stop him becoming television’s famously effortless landscape painter.

Source: [Mental Floss / Bob Ross interviews and production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/art/artists/35-happy-little-facts-about-bob-ross)

### 5. Every Happy Painting Had Two Siblings

Bob Ross usually made three versions of each television painting: a reference, the on-camera work, and another photographed step by step for instructional books.

Source: [Mental Floss / Bob Ross interviews and production history](https://www.mentalfloss.com/art/artists/35-happy-little-facts-about-bob-ross)

The Phantom Menace’s racing machines borrowed real engine recordings, including Porsches and Mustangs. Sound designers reshaped those familiar motors into very different alien vehicles.

### 6. The spaceship hallway really rotated.

For 2001, the Discovery’s centrifuge was built as an enormous rotating set. Clever camera placement made actors appear to walk around an artificial-gravity wheel.

## 95 · World of Tomorrow

### 1. EPCOT was supposed to have residents.

Walt Disney's original EPCOT was a working city where people would actually live. The theme park came later; the city of tomorrow was never built.

Source: [Disney / D23](https://d23.com/a-world-of-tomorrow-inside-walts-last-dream/)

### 2. The house fought the wrecking ball.

Disneyland’s plastic House of the Future proved awkward to demolish. According to Disney’s archive, the wrecking ball bounced off the unusually sturdy structure.

Source: [Disney / D23](https://d23.com/a-to-z/monsanto-house-of-the-future/)

### 3. Tomorrow’s robot took a smoke break.

Elektro, a star of the 1939 World’s Fair, could move its head and arms and smoke cigarettes. The future once arrived with a smoking habit.

Source: [Computer History Museum](https://www.computerhistory.org/revolution/artificial-intelligence-robotics/13/324/1266)

### 4. The future came with a souvenir badge.

After riding General Motors’ Futurama at the 1939 World’s Fair, visitors received an I Have Seen the Future pin. Tomorrow had its own wearable souvenir.

Source: [Smithsonian National Museum of American History](https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_741597)

### 5. A voice played on a keyboard.

The 1939 Voder produced artificial speech through a human operator’s hands and foot pedal. Talking machinery originally needed someone to play it like a complicated instrument.

### 6. Do not open until 6939.

Westinghouse’s World’s Fair time capsule was intended to wait five thousand years before opening. Everyday objects were packed for an audience living in the year 6939.

## 96 · Cliffhangers

### 1. Takeoff was hand-drawn.

The 1948 live-action Superman serial switched to a cartoon Superman for flying effects. An actor could play the Man of Steel; drawings handled the airborne work.

### 2. The Batcave arrived on a movie screen.

Batman’s early film serial helped establish the Batcave before its familiar comic-book form. A low-budget chapter adventure left a permanent room in superhero history.

### 4. The singing cowboy found a lost empire.

Gene Autry’s The Phantom Empire combined cowboy songs with an underground science-fiction civilization. A radio-ranch hero could investigate futuristic danger between musical numbers.

### 5. Superman was not first in line.

Adventures of Captain Marvel brought a superpowered comic-book hero to cinemas in 1941. Its twelve-chapter adventure reached the screen years before the live-action Superman serial.

### 6. Flash borrowed an older rocket.

Flash Gordon’s 1936 serial reused the rocket from Just Imagine, a 1930 science-fiction musical. One studio’s future became another hero’s ride to outer space.

### 5. Kirk was wearing green all along.

Kirk's famous gold command tunic was actually avocado green. Studio lighting and film stock helped turn its color yellow on television.

NASA astronauts Terry Virts and Mike Fincke made guest appearances in the Enterprise finale.

### 7. The finale began in six days.

Ronald D. Moore and Brannon Braga wrote the first draft of All Good Things in six days while also working on the film Star Trek Generations.

### 8. Picard’s vineyard was in California.

The future vineyard scenes in All Good Things were filmed at a winery in Temecula, California. Picard’s retirement looked French with a little geographical assistance.

Source: [StarTrek.com / production history](https://www.startrek.com/news/10-things-you-should-know-about-all-good-things)

### 9. Those whales almost had biblical names.

The Voyage Home’s humpbacks George and Gracie were originally called Adam and Evie. The whales changed names while the story developed through its drafts.

Source: [StarTrek.com / script archives](https://www.startrek.com/news/star-trek-iv-did-you-know-that)

### 10. The aquarium changed its address.

The Voyage Home filmed at Monterey Bay Aquarium, but the story needed Sausalito. The real institution’s name could not be used for its fictional location.

Source: [StarTrek.com / script archives](https://www.startrek.com/news/star-trek-iv-did-you-know-that)

### 11. Even Sarek could be stopped at security.

A cut Voyage Home scene had a guard refuse Sarek entry for lacking credentials. Christine Chapel stepped in to identify the famous Vulcan ambassador.

Source: [StarTrek.com / script archives](https://www.startrek.com/news/star-trek-iv-did-you-know-that)

### 12. The tribbles used to be fuzzies.

David Gerrold first submitted The Trouble with Tribbles as The Fuzzies. The furry creatures kept their reproductive enthusiasm while losing their original name.

Source: [StarTrek.com / script archives](https://www.startrek.com/news/the-fuzzies-celebrating-50-years-of-the-trouble-with-tribbles)

### 13. The writer wrote himself a part.

An early Trouble with Tribbles draft gave Ensign Freeman a job collecting the creatures. David Gerrold had written that small part for himself.

Source: [StarTrek.com / script archives](https://www.startrek.com/news/the-fuzzies-celebrating-50-years-of-the-trouble-with-tribbles)

### 14. Starfleet still had to collect the mail.

An early Tribbles outline sent the Enterprise to the station partly to collect the crew’s mail. Even a starship’s adventures could include an ordinary postal stop.

Source: [StarTrek.com / script archives](https://www.startrek.com/news/the-fuzzies-celebrating-50-years-of-the-trouble-with-tribbles)

### 15. The transporter sparkle was aluminum dust.

Original Star Trek’s transporter glitter was filmed aluminum dust falling through strong light. Effects artists combined those particles with the disappearing actors using separate mattes.

Source: [American Cinematographer / effects artists](https://theasc.com/article/star-trek-50-part-i-original-series-effects/)

### 16. Some stars started black on white.

To create the Enterprise’s rushing star field, effects artists painted black stars on a white background, then combined moving layers at different speeds.

Source: [American Cinematographer / effects artists](https://theasc.com/article/star-trek-50-part-i-original-series-effects/)

### 17. Alien clouds came from a liquid tank.

Star Trek’s strange, churning space formations could be dyes fed into clear liquid. Filming those spreading colors made an ordinary tank look unexpectedly cosmic.

Source: [American Cinematographer / effects artists](https://theasc.com/article/star-trek-50-part-i-original-series-effects/)

### 18. A planet could simply be turned over.

Effects artist Joseph Westheimer described reusing Star Trek planets by changing colors, shifting their positions or turning them upside down. New worlds sometimes began with old footage.

Source: [American Cinematographer / effects artists](https://theasc.com/article/star-trek-50-part-i-original-series-effects/)

### 19. The camera did much of the flying.

For many original Enterprise effects, the camera’s movement created most of the apparent travel. The starship could look fast while the model barely went anywhere.

Source: [American Cinematographer / effects artists](https://theasc.com/article/star-trek-50-part-i-original-series-effects/)

### 20. Vulcan dialogue followed English lips.

A Wrath of Khan scene was filmed in English before becoming Vulcan. Marc Okrand invented syllables that matched the actors’ existing lip movements.

Source: [Marc Okrand / StarTrek.com interview](https://www.startrek.com/news/qapla-klingon-language-creator-marc-okrand-part-1)

### 21. A lunch invitation opened the airlock.

Marc Okrand was in Los Angeles for captioning work when lunch with Paramount friends led to a language job. He had not arrived seeking Star Trek work.

Source: [Marc Okrand / StarTrek.com interview](https://www.startrek.com/news/qapla-klingon-language-creator-marc-okrand-part-1)

### 22. Scotty helped invent Klingon.

James Doohan devised the Klingon lines heard in The Motion Picture. Marc Okrand later treated those sounds as the foundation for a much fuller language.

Source: [Marc Okrand / StarTrek.com interview](https://www.startrek.com/news/qapla-klingon-language-creator-marc-okrand-part-1)

### 23. The language expert also captioned the Oscars.

Before teaching aliens to talk, Marc Okrand helped bring live captions to the 1982 Oscars. His television work connected fictional languages with real accessibility.

Source: [Marc Okrand / StarTrek.com interview](https://www.startrek.com/news/qapla-klingon-language-creator-marc-okrand-part-1)

### 24. An actor’s mistake could change the dictionary.

Marc Okrand sometimes adapted Klingon words to actors’ mispronunciations. A later unsalvageable line was made correct through changes included in the revised dictionary.

Source: [Marc Okrand / StarTrek.com interview](https://www.startrek.com/news/qapla-klingon-language-creator-marc-okrand-part-1)

### 25. Scotty used a hand double.

James Doohan lost a finger after being wounded on D-Day. Carefully framed shots and a hand double helped hide the injury when Scotty operated the controls.

Source: [StarTrek.com / wartime histories](https://www.startrek.com/news/1701-d-day)

### 26. Scotty’s accent had a wartime teacher.

James Doohan learned an Aberdeen soldier’s accent during the war. He later softened that brogue for Scotty so television audiences could understand their engineer.

Source: [StarTrek.com / wartime histories](https://www.startrek.com/news/1701-d-day)

### 27. McCoy once directed planes at Roswell.

Before playing the Enterprise’s doctor, DeForest Kelley worked as a military control-tower operator in Roswell, New Mexico. His earlier station already had a famous sci-fi address.

Source: [StarTrek.com / wartime histories](https://www.startrek.com/news/1701-d-day)

### 28. The poker player really was Hawking.

Data’s poker scene paired actors playing Einstein and Newton with the actual Stephen Hawking. The scientist appeared as a holographic version of himself.

### 29. Lucy’s studio helped launch the Enterprise.

Lucille Ball’s Desilu backed Star Trek through two pilots after the first failed to win a series order. The sitcom star helped make space for science fiction.

### 30. A Talosian also became Cousin Itt.

Felix Silla appeared as a Talosian in The Cage, played Cousin Itt on The Addams Family and later became Twiki in Buck Rogers.

Leonard Nimoy devised the Vulcan nerve pinch for The Enemy Within. The actor replaced a more conventional scripted knockout with a move that felt more like Spock.

During an Orion makeup test, a color timer assumed the green tint was a film problem and corrected it. The deliberately green character looked human again.

### 35. Lwaxana could argue with herself.

Majel Barrett played Lwaxana Troi and voiced the Enterprise computer. In scenes where Lwaxana questions the computer, the actress effectively performs both sides of the conversation.

### 36. The Romulan commander also became G’Kar.

Andreas Katsulas returned as Tomalak while beginning his Babylon 5 run as G’Kar. He had also just played the one-armed man in The Fugitive.
